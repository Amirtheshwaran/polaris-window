    📚 What do these words mean?
    Horizon
    The line where the ground meets the sky. Here it's bumpy because of mountains and craters.
    Elevation (°)
    How high something is above a flat horizon. 0° is level, 90° is straight up. At the south pole the Sun stays within about 1.5° of level.
    Azimuth (°)
    Which direction you're looking, all the way around from 0° to 360°. Here 0° points toward the side of the Moon that faces Earth.
    Lunar day
    One day-night cycle on the Moon lasts about 29.5 Earth days, so the Sun takes a month to go around the horizon once.
    Permanently shadowed region
    A crater floor that sunlight never reaches. It's colder than about −170 °C and can trap water ice.
    Direct-to-Earth (DTE) comms
    Talking to Earth by radio in a straight line. If a hill blocks Earth, the radio link is lost.
    Libration
    The Moon's slight wobble, which makes Earth bob up and down in the lunar sky over about a month.
    Slope
    How tilted the ground is. Landers need gentle ground, usually less than about 8°.

    🌙 Where and when should astronauts land?

    The Moon's south pole has frozen water, but the Sun only peeks over the horizon there, and tall mountains can hide the Sun (no solar power) or the Earth (no radio home).

    Polaris Window uses real NASA maps of the Moon to find the best place and date to land. It works like a weather forecast for the Moon.

    Built with NASA LRO LOLA terrain, NASA/JPL SPICE ephemeris and JPL Horizons, and checked against NASA's published sunlight maps.
